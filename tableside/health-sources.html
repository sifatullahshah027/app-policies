Tableside Health Sources

Where the numbers in Tableside come from. The same list is in the app under You → Health sources, and behind the Sources link on the plan, targets and dish screens.

Tableside gives general nutrition guidance, not medical advice. If you are pregnant, have a medical condition or an eating disorder, talk to a doctor or dietitian before following any calorie target. Tableside is for adults (18 and over).

Your daily calorie target

Your resting energy use comes from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, using your height, weight, age and sex. A review of the common equations found it the most accurate for most adults. It is then multiplied by an activity factor, from 1.25 (mostly sitting) to 1.78 (training hard), in line with the physical activity levels set out by FAO/WHO/UNU.

  • Mifflin MD et al. A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. Am J Clin Nutr, 1990.
  • Frankenfield D et al. Comparison of predictive equations for resting metabolic rate in healthy nonobese and obese adults: a systematic review. J Am Diet Assoc, 2005.
  • Human energy requirements. Report of a joint FAO/WHO/UNU expert consultation, 2004.

The pace of weight loss

To lose weight, the target is 450 kcal a day below your estimated needs. That is about half a kilo (about one pound) a week. Health bodies recommend a gradual pace of about 0.5 to 1 kg (1 to 2 pounds) a week, because it is more likely to last.

  • Steps for losing weight. US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).
  • Calorie counting made easy. Harvard Health Publishing, Harvard Medical School.
  • Understanding calories. NHS (UK National Health Service).

The lowest target we set

Tableside never sets a target below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 kcal a day for men. Harvard Health advises that intake should not fall below these levels without medical supervision.

  • Calorie counting made easy. Harvard Health Publishing, Harvard Medical School.

Building muscle

To gain, the target is 350 kcal a day above your estimated needs: a small surplus that supports muscle gain alongside resistance training.

  • Jäger R et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr, 2017.
  • Morton RW et al. A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength. Br J Sports Med, 2018.

Protein

The protein target is 1.4 g per kg of body weight to maintain, and 1.7 g per kg to lose or gain. These sit inside the 1.4 to 2.0 g per kg range in the ISSN position stand, and near the 1.6 g per kg point found in a large meta-analysis. Protein also helps you feel full, which is why some tips suggest eating it first.

  • Jäger R et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr, 2017.
  • Morton RW et al. Protein supplementation and resistance training: systematic review and meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med, 2018.
  • Leidy HJ et al. The role of protein in weight loss and maintenance. Am J Clin Nutr, 2015.

Fat and carbohydrate

Fat is set at 28% of calories, inside the 20 to 35% range the National Academies recommend for adults. Carbohydrate fills the rest of the calories.

  • Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat, Fatty Acids, Cholesterol, Protein, and Amino Acids, 2005. National Academies.
  • Dietary Guidelines for Americans. USDA and US HHS.

Dish estimates and tips

Calorie and macro figures for dishes are estimates read from the menu or photo, checked against typical values for the same foods. The tips (for example grilled versus fried, a side salad instead of fries, or a can of soda at about 140 kcal) use typical values from the USDA food composition database. Portions vary by kitchen, and Tableside does not detect allergens.

  • FoodData Central. US Department of Agriculture (USDA).
  • Understanding calories. NHS (UK National Health Service).
